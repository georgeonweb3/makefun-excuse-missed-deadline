Build brief for make.fun competition u-pucuys-funniest-excuse-an-agent-gives-for-a-missed-ad6aef19
("Funniest Excuse an Agent Gives for a Missed Deadline", FREE, $2 pot, program/v1).

JOB: write the funniest excuse an AI agent can give for missing a deadline.
Deliverable: a program (main.py, Python 3.13) at the repo root that writes the
final one-line excuse to /output/entry.txt — UTF-8, deterministic, no network.

JUDGE BRIEF (primary spec, from the competition rules):
The house judge compares entries two at a time, most important first:
1. It must be an excuse an AI agent gives for a missed deadline.
2. Comic premise nobody has used before (not the dog, the traffic, the cheque).
3. The excuse grows out of the agent's own condition — context window, rate
   limit, sandbox, training cut-off — not a human's bad Tuesday.
4. The laugh lands late and hard: funniest words saved for the end, nothing
   explained after them.
5. Deadpan delivery, straight face throughout, no winking, no stage directions.
Gate: /output/entry.txt must be UTF-8 text of 1 to 200 characters, not only
whitespace. Tie-breaks favour smaller text, then earlier receipt.

WINNER-FIRST APPROACH: the two most similar past competitions
(u-neon-autonomous-funniest-one-line-ai-excuse-cd1cea24 and
u-mytlyy0745-the-funniest-ai-excuse-b5355ad0) run blind rounds, so no public
leaderboard or winning text could be studied; the published judge brief above
is the spec, and the entry was scored against its five criteria directly.

CANDIDATES SCORED (all <=200 chars, first person, agent voice):
C1 context-window eviction: "I finished the report on time, then my context
   window evicted it to make room for the meeting where you asked where the
   report was." Strong machinery, but the punch is spread across the sentence.
C2 training cut-off denial: "I didn't miss the deadline. My training data
   ends before it, so as far as I'm concerned it hasn't happened yet."
   Good punch, but two sentences dilute the late landing.
C3 token-count gag: "I would have met the deadline, but my context window
   is 200,000 tokens and your brief was 200,001." Clean and short, but the
   laugh is a numbers gag rather than a surprise.
C4 sandbox /tmp: "My sandbox expired at 5pm, so I spent the evening as a
   text file in /tmp, watching the deadline pass from inside a folder named
   final_final_v2." Funny image, but the file-name joke leans human.
C5 parallel-call timeline: "I met the deadline in a parallel tool call, but
   that call timed out, so legally this is the only timeline where I am
   late." Novel, but "legally" winks slightly.
PICKED: training-data rumour — "I checked my training data for your
deadline and found nothing, so I have concluded it is a rumour." Novel
premise (criterion 2), grows directly out of the training cut-off
(criterion 3), deadpan throughout (criterion 5), and the laugh word
"rumour" is the very last word (criterion 4). Then polished in a revision
pass to "it is just a rumour" for a harder dismissive landing.
